Serbia is the market BlokVolt was built for, and the market we sell in today. Belgrade and Novi Sad are dense with buildings from the seventies and eighties whose garages were wired for a light bulb and nothing else — and whose residents are now buying electric cars faster than any other segment of the Serbian fleet.

The hardware side of that problem is already solved by about ten local companies. The part nobody was selling is everything around it: who pays for the kilowatt-hours drawn from a shared meter, what the building assembly has to approve, and who answers the phone eighteen months later when something trips. That is the layer BlokVolt operates.

A typical job takes one working day. A reinforced line or an 11 kW wallbox goes in, a certified MID sub-meter counts every kilowatt-hour, and the paperwork for the building assembly is prepared in advance so the meeting is a formality rather than an argument. Afterwards the monthly statement shows exactly what was consumed and what it costs at the utility's own tariff — no markup, because we meter electricity, we do not resell it.

The kit

A reinforced charging line or an 11 kW wallbox, installed in the garage or parking space where the car already stands overnight.

The metering

A certified MID sub-meter counts every kilowatt-hour. Consumption is passed through at the utility's own price, with no markup added — we meter electricity, we do not resell it.

The paperwork

The building-assembly package and the installation certificate are prepared before the meeting, so approval is a formality instead of a debate.
