What we actually sell

Ten companies sell chargers. Nobody was selling the rest.

Hardware is the easy part of home charging in a building. The hard part is shared electricity, a building assembly that has to say yes, and support after the invoice is paid. BlokVolt is built around those three.

01

Metering, not reselling

A certified MID sub-meter records consumption; the resident is billed at the price on the utility's own invoice, without a markup. That distinction is what keeps the model clean — an electricity trading licence is for people who sell energy, and we do not.

02

The building assembly package

Notice, agenda wording, the technical annex and the installation certificate, prepared before the meeting. Most refusals happen because nobody could answer a neighbour's question about the shared bill — so we answer it in writing, in advance.

03

A subscription that has a job

A monthly statement you can show your neighbours, alerts when something behaves oddly, service within 48 hours, a winter check, an extended warranty and a free relocation if you move. Optional, cancellable, no lock-in.

Why Serbia first

A small fleet growing fast, a housing stock dominated by apartment buildings, and a purchase subsidy that keeps pulling new drivers in. All figures below are official Serbian sources, as of spring 2026.

7,155battery electric cars registered in Serbia, April 2026 (Ministry of the Interior, via RTS)
+221%year-on-year growth in EV registrations, first quarter of 2026
~40,000electric vehicles targeted by 2030 in the national energy and climate plan
€5,000state subsidy per battery electric car, budgeted again for 2026

Roughly 40–50% of those drivers park somewhere without a socket of their own — a garage, a box, an assigned space in a shared building. That is the addressable market, and it is the one nobody is serving properly.
